# Paradigms without a language implementation

## A necessary distinction

There are few **models of computation**, and they are mathematical: the lambda calculus, the Turing
machine, term rewriting, resolution, Petri nets, the pi-calculus, dataflow, cellular
automata, interaction nets, linear logic. In seventy years only a handful have been added.

There are many **paradigms**, but they are not independent. Van Roy's map of two dozen-odd paradigms
is generated by a small kernel plus or minus a few orthogonal concepts: named
state, nondeterminism, concurrency, search with backtracking, unification, laziness.

**The impostor test:** if an invention can be described as "kernel + state +
channels", it already exists.

---

## Models with a theory but without a language

- **Petri nets.** The theory has been developed in great depth, and there are dozens of modeling tools, yet there is practically no programming language in which a Petri net is the execution model. Everything that exists is modeling, not programming. A gaping hole.
- **The chemical abstract machine (CHAM) and the Gamma language.** A program is a multiset, computation is reactions, there is no order at all, parallelism is absolute and free. The formalism is published; there are essentially no implementations. The most radical of the unfinished ones.
- **Membrane computing (P systems).** A hierarchy of membranes, rules inside, everything simultaneous. There are simulators, no language.
- **Linear logic as the basis of a language.** A resource cannot be copied or discarded: a property of the logic, not an annotation. Ownership in Rust is a pale shadow. Full-fledged languages exist only in research.
- **Reflective towers.** Brian Cantwell Smith's 3-Lisp: a program rises to the level of the interpreter that runs it, changes it and comes back down, and so on infinitely upward. Implementations can be counted on one hand (3-Lisp, Brown, Blond, Black), all old. **The most mind-blowing and the smallest in terms of work.**
- **Executable temporal logic** (Tempura, MetateM). You write not how, but when what must be true. Almost unimplemented.
- **Amorphous and spatial computing** (Abelson, MIT; Proto, Protelis). You program a continuous medium; the behavior of an element is derived. A few academic languages, zero practice.
- **Interaction nets** (Lafont, 1990) are **the telling exception**: the model lay unused for thirty years, and recently a working runtime with automatic parallelism was built on it (HVM / Bend). The best proof that the approach works.

---

## Paradigms with implementations that were forgotten

- **Linda and tuple spaces** (Gelernter). Coordination as the only primitive: processes do not know about each other, they communicate through a shared space, taking and putting tuples by pattern. Died almost completely, and quite undeservedly: a ready-made model for distributed systems, more honest than queues with topics.
- **Synchronous languages**: Esterel, Lustre, Signal. They live in avionics, where airplanes fly on them, and are unknown outside it. Time is discrete, reaction is instantaneous, behavior is deterministic and verifiable.
- **Lucid / intensional programming.** A variable's value depends on a context dimension; a program is a function of a point in the space of contexts.
- **The pi-calculus as a language**: Pict died. The join calculus gave rise to several languages; the ideas seeped into the mainstream and lost their originality.
- **Unison**: content-addressed code (by hash, without names), an example of a recently implemented new paradigm.

## Can we invent our own

Out of nothing, no, and the attempts are visible right away.

**The mechanism by which paradigms are actually born: a paradigm appears when
the cost structure of the hardware changes.** Functional programming waited for cheap memory and many
cores. Object orientation spread when not the programs but the teams grew. Everything related
to dataflow came from accelerators.

The question is reformulated: what is getting more expensive and cheaper now, and what has no notation.

### Four honest candidates

1. **Energy.** Watts have become scarcer than cycles, but in no language is the cost in joules part of the type; it is always a property of the implementation that people learn about from the electricity bill. A language in which the energy cost of an expression is checked by the compiler as a type **does not exist**. The most honest candidate for a new paradigm; it connects to reversible computing, where erasing a bit has a physical cost per Landauer. **There are no predecessors to catch up with and no risk of reinvention. There is a customer: anyone who pays for a rack.**
2. **Location and movement.** Inside the chassis there is already a distributed system, and languages pretend that memory is one and close by. Emerald could make moving an object a property of the language. It is needed more today than it was then.
3. **Failure.** At scale partial failure is the norm, but in every language it remains a library topic. Argus tried to bring it into the semantics and was forgotten. A paradigm in which non-delivery and partial failure are expressed in types rather than in try-catch has not been built.
4. **Machine authorship.** If the main author of code stops being a human, there is no reason to optimize notation for human reading. A program = intent + evidence of correctness, and the text becomes a derived artifact. A direct consequence of what is happening; there is no notation.

---

## What to do with this

- **The cheapest and most striking:** a reflective tower. A couple of thousand lines, a week, and the demonstration breaks your picture of the world. Forgotten, implementable, explainable on your fingers.
- **The most useful for track 4:** the tuple space as a language, not a library. It lands in the same place as Refal with rewriting rules; possibly the same idea from another side.
- **The most original:** energy in the type system. It needs to be broken down to the level of "what it looks like syntactically and what the compiler checks"; there it will quickly become clear whether the idea works or falls apart. Finding out is cheap.
